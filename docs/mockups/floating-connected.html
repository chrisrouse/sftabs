Connecting the handle to the menu

Today the handle and the panel are separate surfaces, each with its own border and shadow, so they read as two objects that happen to touch. And a white panel on a white Salesforce card needs more than a hairline to separate from it. Each option below puts the elevation on the whole assembly instead of the parts. Toggle open/closed to compare.

1 · Seamless tab

One surface, one shadow, corners rounded only on the outside of the whole unit. The seam between handle and panel is a single hairline, so the handle reads as a tab protruding from the menu rather than a separate chip. Most subtle; relies on the shadow alone to lift off the page.

2 · Nubbin

The handle stays its own rounded shape but grows a pointer into the panel, the way a tooltip or popover attaches to its trigger. The strongest "these belong together" signal, and it makes clear which control opened the menu. Slightly more decoration to maintain across four anchor sides.

3 · Active handle

The handle fills with brand blue while the menu is open and the panel carries a matching accent edge, so the pair share an obvious "on" state. Also gives the clearest closed/open feedback of the four. The blue is a strong note on a Salesforce page — worth judging in context.
